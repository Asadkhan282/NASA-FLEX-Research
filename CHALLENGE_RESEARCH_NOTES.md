# Research reading notes — NASA Space Apps 2026

## Challenge framing (summary only)

**Flame in Freefall: AI-Powered Fire Safety Insights from Microgravity Combustion Data** asks for an interactive AI-powered dashboard able to organize and interpret microgravity combustion findings for human space-exploration fire safety. The full challenge statement is expected October 28, 2026. This is a planning brief, not a build specification and not a claim that any particular NASA dataset is appropriate.

Official summary: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/flame-in-freefall-ai-powered-fire-safety-insights-from-microgravity-combustion-data/

## Questions to answer once full challenge drops (October 28)

- Which specific datasets, file formats, and units does the challenge recommend?
- What constitutes a valid comparative ranking of heterogeneous experiments?
- What evidence standards and traceable citations will the judges expect?
- Which findings are observational versus mechanistic predictions?
- Does the challenge ask for AI summarization, retrieval, predictive ML, or a combination?
- How will model hallucinations and unsupported safety advice be prevented?
- Are the data files downloadable without an account and legally redistributable?
- What are the demo, submission, and citation rules?

## Skills rehearsal is not competition solution work

The companion generic dashboard uses electronics bench data invented for learning. Do not transplant its files directly as submission work without explicit approval from the event organizers regarding prior-created assets.
